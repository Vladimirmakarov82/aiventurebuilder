Stage 1 · The first 15 partners

Work with clients starts with a gift: a three-month subscription to the Orbiq concierge

  1. 01The partner’s clients get a gift: 3 months of a service that costs $10,000 a year. The clients stay with the partner.
  2. 02Other partners (at least 15 at launch) learn each other’s products and offer their clients what is relevant to them, widening the value they bring. When needed, they pass on the client’s contact.
  3. 03The partner earns on other partners’ services for its clients. Orbiq AI helps choose what to offer a client or which questions to ask.
  4. 04An Orbiq subscription costs a client $10,000 a year. Every client who pays for the Orbiq subscription brings the partner $3,000 a year.

The Orbiq network has at most 2 partners per service segment.

  • $10,000 + $30,000 at IPOPre-IPO. A client puts $1M into a company before its IPO through a fund in the network. The fund takes 5% at entry, $50,000, and 15% of the profit at exit. The company doubles: the client makes $1M, the fund’s share is $150,000. The partner gets 20% of both.
  • $45,000Business sale. A client sells a company for $5M. An M&A advisor in the network charges about $225,000 on a deal this size and pays the partner 20%.
  • $40,000 a yearWealth management. A client moves $20M to a manager in the network. The manager charges 1% a year, $200,000. The partner gets 20% every year the client stays.
  • $20,000Citizenship for the family. The client’s family gets a second citizenship. A licensed agent in the network charges about $100,000 for the full process and pays the partner 20%.
  • $8,000 + $3,000 a yearTrust for heirs. A client asked the partner how to pass capital to the children. The partner introduced a trust company from the network. A Jersey trust costs about $40,000 to set up and about $15,000 a year to run. The company pays the partner 20%.
  • $20,000Painting. An art advisor in the network sources a $1M work and takes 10%, $100,000. The partner gets 20%.
  • $62,500Villa. A client buys a $5M house. The agent earns 5%, $250,000, and by market norm pays 25% to whoever brought the buyer.

Total: $208,500 in the first year. $43,000 of it repeats every year: the trust and wealth management. The partner gets another $30,000 when the company goes public. The partner does the deals: asks clients questions, makes introductions, follows through. Orbiq provides the rest: vetted partners with unique services, no more than two per segment, fee agreements, and its own audience of wealthy clients. That audience also comes to the partner for the partner’s own service.

Market rates: pre-IPO funds take 1–10% at entry and 10–20% of profit, an M&A advisor earns about $225,000 on average on a $5M deal (Firmex survey), citizenship agents get $12,000–50,000 per application, real estate agents pass on 25% of the commission for a client, art advisors take 10%, a complex Jersey trust costs £30–50k to set up.

Partner requirements

Who gets into the network No more than two partners per segment

  • 01A unique product or service. Own access, own master, own technology or own geography that others in the segment lack.
  • 02Stories worth telling. Deals for well-known and influential clients, hard problems, results with numbers. Three such stories go into the Orbiq journal.
  • 03HNWI experience. Clients with $1M+ in capital and knowing how to work with them.
  • 04Clean reputation. Licenses where required, no client disputes, compliance check passed.
  • 05A 20–25% fee on client revenue to whoever brought the client, under the network agreement.
  • 06Reply to a client within 24 hours and full confidentiality.
